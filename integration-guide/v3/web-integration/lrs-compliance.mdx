<Info>
  **LRS** is the Liberalised Remittance Scheme — the RBI rules for money sent abroad by an **individual** in India, for things like education fees or travel. It does not apply to a business paying a business.

  This page covers **education** payments, on purpose codes `S0305` and `S1107`. Collection, the payment lifecycle, webhooks, and settlement are the same whatever you collect for — see [Collection Overview](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/collection-overview).
</Info>

When an individual in India sends money abroad, LRS decides whether they are allowed to, how much, what tax applies, and what paperwork is needed. EximPe is licensed by the RBI as a **Payment Aggregator – Cross Border**, and a partner bank makes the actual transfer.

You give us the buyer, the amount, and the documents. We enforce the rest.

<Note>
  FEMA, TCS, PAN, `₹10,00,000` — if any of the terms on this page are unfamiliar, the [Glossary](/integration-guide/v3/getting-started/glossary) has them all.
</Note>

| You | EximPe |
| :--- | :--- |

| Identify the buyer, collect their details and documents | Check the PAN, the documents, and the declaration |

  Onboard each institution you collect for as its own sub-merchant, with its own settlement details. Collections are tracked per sub-merchant. You can onboard one or many.
</Note>

## The rules in four lines

- **Who can pay** — resident individuals only, including minors through a guardian. Not companies, partnerships, HUFs, trusts, or non-residents.
- **How much** — USD 250,000 per person per financial year, 1 April to 31 March. That ceiling covers everything they send through every bank, not just through us.
- **Whose money** — it must come from the buyer's own account. LRS is reported against the PAN of the account that was debited.
- **What for** — every payment carries a FEMA purpose code, and the code decides the documents and the tax.

## What we check

<Steps>
  <Step title="They are a resident individual">
    Verifying the PAN rejects company, HUF, and firm PANs automatically. Residency cannot be proved by PAN alone — non-residents hold individual PANs too — so the buyer also affirms it in the declaration.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The money came from their own account">
    LRS runs on the PAN of the debited account, so the sender must match the buyer you named. A payment always settles against whoever actually paid; it can never be booked to a different person's PAN.

    Where a parent or sponsor pays for a student, **the parent is the buyer** — their PAN, their account — and the relationship to the student is declared.

    If the money arrives from an account that does not match, the payment is held. You then either refund it, or send us the PAN of whoever actually paid so it settles against them and their limit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They affirmed the declaration">
    The buyer affirms it; you pass the result to us.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The declaration

Three requirements — resident status, the LRS limit, and source of funds — are put to the buyer as **one declaration**. They must affirm it for the payment to proceed.

> "I am a resident individual under FEMA, and this remittance is under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme. My total LRS this financial year, including this remittance, is within the permitted limit of USD 250,000, and it is for a permitted purpose (not one prohibited under FEMA/RBI). The funds are from my own legitimate sources and comply with FEMA, RBI, AML and income-tax laws. The information in this declaration is true and complete and I accept sole responsibility for its accuracy."

It maps onto a single field, `buyer_declaration`, on [Submit Verification Details](/api-reference/v3/lrs/submit-verification-details). Send `true` once the buyer has affirmed it.

<Note>
  Read the exact wording to show the buyer from the requirement set — [Get Verification Requirements](/api-reference/v3/lrs/verification-requirements) returns it as the `label` on `buyer_declaration`. Use that rather than copying the text above, so your screen stays right when the wording is revised.
</Note>

## Tax at source (TCS)

**2% on whatever the buyer sends abroad above ₹10,00,000 in a financial year.** The first ₹10 lakh each year is tax-free.

**EximPe works the figure out.** There is nothing for you or the buyer to declare — we compute it against our record of what that PAN has sent through us this financial year. If this payment is the one that crosses ₹10 lakh, only the part above the threshold is taxed.

[Quote LRS Amount](/api-reference/v3/lrs/quote) returns the exact figure. Collect it on top of the amount to be settled. The bank pays it to the government against the buyer's PAN, and the buyer claims it back as a tax credit — it shows up in their Form 26AS.

<Note>
  We track LRS usage and **refuse anything that would breach the USD 250,000 annual cap**, on the data we can see. The bank is the wider backstop at settlement.
</Note>

## Purpose codes and documents

Two codes, both for education paid from the buyer's own funds. Loan-funded remittances are not supported yet.

| Purpose code | What it is for |
| :--- | :--- |
| `S0305` | Studying abroad — tuition, where the student usually travels |
| `S1107` | Education fees with no travel involved |

Documents are decided by the purpose code. For education:

| Document | `S0305` | `S1107` |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Letter of admission (`loa`) | Required | Required |
| Student passport (`student_passport`) | Required | — |
| Student visa (`student_visa`) | Required¹ | — |
| University ID card (`university_id_card`) | Required¹ | Required |
| Invoice (`invoice`) | Optional | Optional |
| Relationship declaration (`relationship_declaration`) | If the buyer is not the student | If the buyer is not the student |

¹ Alternatives — supplying either one clears both. `S1107` has no travel leg, so there is no visa and the university ID card stands alone.

<Warning>
  **Do not build a form off this table.** It is here so you know roughly what to ask a buyer for. The authoritative set for a given payment — including the travel codes, and anything added as the rules change — comes from [Get Verification Requirements](/api-reference/v3/lrs/verification-requirements).
</Warning>

<Info>
  No scanned PAN card. PAN is a field, validated electronically.
</Info>

## What partners get wrong

- **The money comes from someone else's account.** Enforce own-account payment in your UX. Where a parent pays, onboard the parent as the buyer.
- **Treating the student as the buyer.** Capture the student separately, and set `relation` to `SELF` only when they really are the same person.
- **Quoting before verifying the PAN.** An inactive, non-individual, or name-mismatched PAN is a guaranteed rejection later. Catch it first.
- **Showing the buyer a price without the tax.** They underpay, and you handle a shortfall.
- **Poor document scans.** Legible, in date, right type. The offer letter is the one that matters most.
