A webhook is a POST we send to your server when something happens — money arrived, a payment settled, a refund went through.

You need them. There is no polling endpoint that tells you a credit landed, so without a webhook URL you will not know your buyer has paid.

## Events we send

| Event | We send it when | You do this |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Merchant Approved** | A sub-merchant is approved | Start creating accounts and collecting for them |
| **Payment Successful** | Money arrives and we create a payment | Begin the compliance step |
| **LRS Verification Needed** | An LRS payment still needs something — named fields, named documents, or more money | Send what it names — see [LRS Verification](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/lrs-verification) |
| **Verification Needed** | A non-LRS payment needs details, or its verification becomes `IN_REVIEW` or `EXPIRED`. A **verified** payment sends nothing — there is nothing to act on | Fix what `action_required_fields` names, or record the outcome |
| **Payment Under Review** | An LRS payment's verification was accepted and it needs nothing further from you | Stop chasing the payer; wait for Payment Settled |
| **Verification Status** | A filed LRS, B2B services or B2B goods verification is reviewed and approved or rejected | Record the verdict; on a rejection, show the payer what the documents name |
| **Payment Failed** | A credit could not be processed | Investigate and reconcile |
| **Payment Settled** | Money is settled to you | Update your books |
| **Payment Refunded** | A refund goes through | Update your ledger, tell the buyer |
| **Refund / Dispute Updates** | A refund status changes or a dispute moves | Track it |

Payload for each is in the [API Reference](/api-reference/v3/webhooks/payment-successful).

<Warning>
  **LRS payments get `LRS_VERIFICATION_NEEDED`, not `VERIFICATION_NEEDED`.** If you collect education fees and you only handle `VERIFICATION_NEEDED`, your handler will never fire.
</Warning>

<Note>
  A verified payment sends nothing. `VERIFIED` means nothing is outstanding, so there is no event — do not wait on one to confirm a payment passed.
</Note>

## Set it up

**1. Build the endpoint.** A public HTTPS URL that accepts `POST` with `application/json` and returns `200 OK`.

**2. Register it.** In your EximPe Dashboard, go to **Developer** and enter the URL. If you cannot use the dashboard, send the URL and your server IP to EximPe Support instead.

**3. Whitelist our IPs.** The ranges are in the same Developer section. Configure your firewall to accept webhooks only from those addresses.

**4. Test it.** Make a sandbox payment, watch it arrive, confirm you return `200`.

## Driving every event in sandbox

You do not have to wait for real money, or a real settlement date, to exercise your handlers. Sandbox lets you fire each event on demand.

| To test | Call |
| :--- | :--- |
| Money arriving, and the verification that follows | [Simulate VBA Transfer](/api-reference/v3/vba/simulate-transfer) |
| `PAYMENT_SETTLED`, with a real settlement record and fee arithmetic | [Mark as Settled](/api-reference/v3/settlement/mark-settled) |
| `PAYMENT_REFUNDED`, `REFUND_FAILED`, `REFUND_STATUS_UPDATE` | [Set Refund Status](/api-reference/v3/refunds/simulate-status) |

Set Refund Status is the useful one for coverage: force a single refund through `REFUNDED`, `FAILED`, `PROCESSING`, `REVERSED`, `ON_HOLD` and `BANK_REJECTED_REFUND` in turn, and one refund exercises every branch of your handler. `INITIATED` fires nothing — it is the starting state, not a transition.

<Warning>
  **Both need the Simulator gateway.** They only work on transactions created through it, and return `400` in production. If your sandbox account is not on the Simulator gateway, ask your EximPe integration manager to assign it.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Mark as Settled applies a **simulated** fee schedule, not your contracted rates. Use it to check that your reconciliation arithmetic holds, not to predict what a production settlement will pay you.
</Note>

## Check the message is really from us

Every webhook carries an HMAC-SHA256 signature. Verify it before you act on anything — otherwise anyone who finds your URL can tell you a payment succeeded.

There are two headers, and you get both on every delivery:

| Header | Signed material |
| :--- | :--- |
| `X-Webhook-Signature-V2` | `HMAC-SHA256(encryption_key, "<timestamp>." + raw_body)`, sent as `t=<timestamp>,v2=<hex>` |
| `X-Webhook-Signature` | `HMAC-SHA256(encryption_key, raw_body)`, hex |

<Tip>
  **Prefer V2.** Its timestamp is signed along with the body, so a captured delivery can't be replayed later with a fresh one. Split the header on `,`, take `t` and `v2`, rebuild the hash over `t + "." + raw_body`, and reject anything whose `t` is too old. Both headers are sent on every delivery, so you can verify either. The walk-through below covers V1.
</Tip>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Take the signature from the header">
    Read `X-Webhook-Signature` off the request.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get your Encryption Key">
    It is in the Developer section of the dashboard.

    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/encryption-key.png" alt="Get your Encryption Key from Developer Section" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rebuild the signature">
    HMAC-SHA256, using your Encryption Key as the secret and the **raw** request body as the message. Output as a hex string.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare">
    They must match exactly. Use a timing-safe comparison.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **Use the raw body, not a re-serialised object.** If you parse the JSON and stringify it again, your bytes will differ from ours and the signature will never match. If you have no choice, serialise with keys sorted at every level, no spaces, and non-ASCII characters escaped as `\uXXXX` — `json.dumps(data, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":"))`. A number we sent as `1180.0` comes back as `1180` in some languages, so rebuilding the bytes is not reliable for every payload; keep the raw body when you can.
</Warning>

```javascript Node.js
const crypto = require('crypto');

function verifyWebhookSignature(payload, signature, encryptionKey) {
  // Create HMAC using your Encryption Key
  const hmac = crypto.createHmac('sha256', encryptionKey);
  
  // Update with the raw JSON body
  hmac.update(payload, 'utf8');
  
  // Generate the expected signature
  const expectedSignature = hmac.digest('hex');
  
  // Compare signatures
  return crypto.timingSafeEqual(
    Buffer.from(signature, 'hex'),
    Buffer.from(expectedSignature, 'hex')
  );
}

// Sort keys at every nesting level
function sortKeys(value) {
  if (Array.isArray(value)) return value.map(sortKeys);
  if (value && typeof value === 'object') {
    return Object.keys(value).sort().reduce((sorted, key) => {
      sorted[key] = sortKeys(value[key]);
      return sorted;
    }, {});
  }
  return value;
}

function generateSignatureFromObject(webhookData, encryptionKey) {
  // If you need to recreate signature from a JSON object
  // Use the same serialization as EximPe: sorted keys, no spaces, non-ASCII escaped
  const payloadJson = JSON.stringify(sortKeys(webhookData))
    .replace(/[\u0080-\uffff]/g, (c) => '\\u' + c.charCodeAt(0).toString(16).padStart(4, '0'));
  
  const hmac = crypto.createHmac('sha256', encryptionKey);
  hmac.update(payloadJson, 'utf8');
  return hmac.digest('hex');
}

// Usage in Express.js
app.post('/webhook', express.raw({ type: 'application/json' }), (req, res) => {
  const signature = req.headers['x-webhook-signature'];
  const payload = req.body.toString();
  
  if (!verifyWebhookSignature(payload, signature, process.env.ENCRYPTION_KEY)) {
    return res.status(401).send('Invalid signature');
  }
  
  // Process webhook
  const webhookData = JSON.parse(payload);
  console.log('Webhook verified:', webhookData);
  
  res.status(200).send('OK');
});
```

```python Python
import hmac
import hashlib
import json

def verify_webhook_signature(payload, signature, encryption_key):
    # Create HMAC using your Encryption Key
    expected_signature = hmac.new(
        encryption_key.encode('utf-8'),
        payload.encode('utf-8'),
        hashlib.sha256
    ).hexdigest()
    
    # Compare signatures securely
    return hmac.compare_digest(signature, expected_signature)

def generate_signature_from_object(webhook_data, encryption_key):
    # If you need to recreate signature from a JSON object
    # Use the same serialization as EximPe
    payload_json = json.dumps(
        webhook_data, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":")
    )
    signature = hmac.new(
        encryption_key.encode(), payload_json.encode(), hashlib.sha256
    ).hexdigest()
    return signature
```

```php PHP
function verifyWebhookSignature($payload, $signature, $encryptionKey) {
    // Create HMAC using your Encryption Key
    $expectedSignature = hash_hmac('sha256', $payload, $encryptionKey);
    
    // Compare signatures securely
    return hash_equals($expectedSignature, $signature);
}

// Sort keys at every nesting level; lists keep their order
function sortKeysRecursive($value) {
    if (!is_array($value)) {
        return $value;
    }
    foreach ($value as $key => $item) {
        $value[$key] = sortKeysRecursive($item);
    }
    if ($value && array_keys($value) !== range(0, count($value) - 1)) {
        ksort($value, SORT_STRING);
    }
    return $value;
}

function generateSignatureFromObject($webhookData, $encryptionKey) {
    // If you need to recreate signature from a JSON object
    // Use the same serialization as EximPe: sorted keys, no spaces, non-ASCII escaped
    $payloadJson = json_encode(sortKeysRecursive($webhookData), JSON_UNESCAPED_SLASHES);
    
    return hash_hmac('sha256', $payloadJson, $encryptionKey);
}

// Usage in Laravel/Symfony
public function webhook(Request $request) {
    $signature = $request->header('X-Webhook-Signature');
    $payload = $request->getContent();
    
    if (!verifyWebhookSignature($payload, $signature, config('app.encryption_key'))) {
        return response('Invalid signature', 401);
    }
    
    // Process webhook
    $webhookData = json_decode($payload, true);
    Log::info('Webhook verified:', $webhookData);
    
    return response('OK', 200);
}
```

## Delivery and duplicates

Delivery is at-least-once, so the same event can reach you more than once.

- Every event carries a unique `sequence_number`. It is a UUID, not an ordering counter. A retry resends the same value, so de-duplicate on it.
- Do not rely on arrival order. Order events by `event_time`, and when it matters call [Get Verification Requirements](/api-reference/v3/lrs/verification-requirements) for where the payment stands now.
- `event_time` is when we created the event, not when we delivered it.

## If your endpoint is down

We retry. A delivery fails when your endpoint answers with anything other than a `2xx`, does not answer within **10 seconds**, or cannot be reached. After a failed attempt we try again **1 minute** later, then **5 minutes**, **15 minutes** and **1 hour** after each further failure, **5 attempts** in all. After the fifth failure we stop sending that event.

Return `200` as soon as you have stored the event, and do your processing afterwards.

<Note>
  **Verification asks are batched.** [`VERIFICATION_NEEDED`](/api-reference/v3/webhooks/verification-needed) and [`LRS_VERIFICATION_NEEDED`](/api-reference/v3/webhooks/lrs-verification-needed) are queued per merchant and sent in batches every **2 minutes**, so the first attempt can come a couple of minutes later, and longer behind a burst. Their retries ride the same batches. Every other event is sent as soon as it is created.
</Note>

<Warning>
  **Four things to get right.**

  - Never expose your encryption key.
  - Verify every signature.
  - HTTPS only.
  - Log what you receive — you will need it to reconcile.
</Warning>
