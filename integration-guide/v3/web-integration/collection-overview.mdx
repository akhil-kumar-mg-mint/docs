Your Indian buyer pays in rupees. You send us the compliance details. We settle the money to your account abroad.

This page shows the whole flow. Read it once, then follow the linked pages to build.

## The five steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create your merchants">
    PSPs create a sub-merchant for each business they collect for. Wait for approval before collecting.

    [Create Merchant](/api-reference/v3/merchant/create) · [Merchant Approved webhook](/api-reference/v3/webhooks/merchant-approved)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set up a way to get paid">
    Two ways to collect: send the buyer to an EximPe-hosted payment page, or give them a virtual account to pay into directly.

    [Hosted Checkout](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/hosted-checkout) · [Virtual Bank Accounts](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/virtual-accounts)
  </Step>
  <Step title="The buyer pays">
    We create a payment and send you a webhook. Its status is `ACTION_REQUIRED`, because we do not have the compliance details yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the compliance details">
    Tell us who paid, what for, and attach the documents. The status becomes `IN_REVIEW`.

    For a B2C payment, the `buyer`, `product`, and `invoice` fields you already sent at [Create Order](/api-reference/v3/order/create) are enough — there's nothing further to send.

    A Virtual Account credit arrives with no Create Order call behind it, so those same basic facts get filled in afterward in the EximPe dashboard instead.

    For LRS and B2B services payments we tell you exactly what is needed, you send it, and it completes itself. An **LRS** payment (an individual paying for education or travel) verifies the buyer's PAN and collects the tax figures — see [LRS Verification](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/lrs-verification). A **B2B services** payment (a business paying for a service) has no identity step and no tax at all — see [B2B Services Verification](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/b2b-services-verification).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get your money">
    We convert the amount to be settled at the exchange rate on the settlement date and send it to your account abroad. Status becomes `SETTLED`. Our fee, the GST on it, and any tax were paid by the buyer on top, so the whole amount reaches you.

    [What it costs you](/integration-guide/v3/getting-started/faqs#what-does-it-cost-me) · [Settlement APIs](/api-reference/v3/settlement/list) · [Payment Settled webhook](/api-reference/v3/webhooks/payment-settled)
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How the buyer pays

Money can reach you by more than one Indian payment method.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Hosted Checkout" icon="credit-card" href="/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/hosted-checkout">
    Create an order, send the buyer to EximPe's payment page, and they pay by card, UPI, net banking, or QR. Nothing sensitive touches your servers.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Virtual Bank Accounts" icon="building-columns" href="/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/virtual-accounts">
    An Indian account number we issue for you. The buyer pays into it by NEFT, RTGS, or IMPS, exactly as they would pay any local supplier. Give them the account number and the IFSC.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  **The method only decides how the money arrives.** Once a payment exists it behaves the same way whichever route it came by — same statuses, same webhooks, same compliance step, same settlement.
</Note>

## Who is who

| Term | What it means |
| :--- | :--- |
| **PSP** | You, if you collect on behalf of other businesses. You onboard them, you call the API for them, and we settle their money to you. |
| **Sub-merchant** | A business onboarded by a PSP — a university, a hospital, a travel operator. Collections are tracked against it, and its PSP pays it onward. |
| **Direct merchant** | You, if you collect only for yourself. We settle straight to you. |
| **Buyer** | Whoever in India is paying you. In LRS rules and API fields they are the **remitter**, identified by PAN — `remitter_pan`, `remitter_relation`, and the remitter lock on an account. |

<Warning>
  One virtual account takes payments from **many** buyers. So a credit landing does not tell you who paid — you tell us, in step 4.
</Warning>

## The two amounts

These are not the same number, and mixing them up is the most common way a payment stalls.

| Term | What it is | In the API |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Amount to be collected** | What you ask the buyer to transfer. The amount to be settled, plus our fee, the GST on that fee, and any tax. | `total_payable_inr` on the quote |
| **Amount to be settled** | What reaches you abroad. This is the figure the buyer's payment is actually *for*. | `amount_to_be_settled` on submit |

Ask for the amount to be collected. Declare the amount to be settled. Collect the smaller of the two and the payment sits short until the buyer tops up.

<Note>
  **Which flow this applies to.** Declaring `amount_to_be_settled` and quoting the tax on it belongs to the LRS flow. A B2B services payment declares no amount at all: what settles is the credit itself, read from the payment.
</Note>

## Payment statuses

| `verification_status` | What it means | What you do |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ACTION_REQUIRED` | We have the money but not everything we need — either you have not sent the details yet, or something you sent did not pass | Read what is outstanding, fix it, send again |
| `IN_REVIEW` | Details accepted, bank is processing | Wait |
| `VERIFIED` | Nothing outstanding. No webhook fires for this. | Nothing |
| `EXPIRED` | The window closed. Terminal — the money returns to the buyer. | Nothing to recover |

Settlement is reported separately, by the settlement APIs and `PAYMENT_SETTLED`.

<Warning>
  **LRS payments work to a deadline.** Each carries a verification expiry. Miss it and the payment expires — terminal, and the money goes back to the buyer. See [The payment has a deadline](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/lrs-verification#the-payment-has-a-deadline).
</Warning>

## When things go wrong

**The buyer paid too little.** The payment waits, and the `outstanding` entry on the event names the shortfall. Either have the buyer transfer the difference and link that credit as a top-up with `linked_payment_id`, or declare a smaller `amount_to_be_settled` for what actually arrived and submit again.

**A document was rejected.** We tell you exactly what is wrong. Fix only that item and send it again.

**The money came from someone else's account.** This applies to LRS payments only. The name on the sending account did not match the PAN you gave us, so the payment stays in `action_required` with `reason: SENDER_NAME_MISMATCH`. Either we refund it and the buyer pays again from their own account, or you send us the PAN of whoever actually paid — the payment then settles against that person and counts against their LRS limit.

**You need to refund.** Refunds go back to the buyer's own account. You can refund part of a payment, more than once. If your balance does not cover it, the call fails — collect more and try again.

<Note>
  **Where refund money comes from.** A refund is netted against your upcoming settlements. We hold a balance for each merchant, and the refund comes out of money we have not settled to you yet. If that balance is too small the call is rejected, so retry once you have collected more.
</Note>
