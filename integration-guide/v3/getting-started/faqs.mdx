## General

### What is this product?

A cross-border collection rail. An Indian buyer pays rupees into a virtual account we issue for you, you supply the compliance details the payment needs, and the funds are settled to the beneficiary abroad under the RBI **Payment Aggregator – Cross Border (PA-CB)** framework. A bank partner — an **Authorised Dealer**, or "AD bank" — performs the actual outward remittance.

### Does EximPe show my buyer a payment page?

Not today. Virtual bank accounts are driven entirely from your own product, so the buyer stays in your experience throughout — they just make a bank transfer. Methods where EximPe hosts the buyer-facing page are on the roadmap; contact your KAM about what is available to you.

### How does the buyer actually pay?

By a standard domestic bank transfer — **NEFT, RTGS, or IMPS** — into the virtual bank account you share with them. No SWIFT, no international wire, no intermediary banks.

### What can I collect for?

Every payment carries a FEMA **purpose code**, and the purpose code decides the compliance rules — who may pay, which documents are needed, and what tax applies. EximPe supports **LRS education** payments today, on purpose codes `S0305` and `S1107`.

Your account is enabled for a specific set of purpose codes (its allowlist). To collect for a purpose outside it, contact your EximPe KAM.

### Does the payment type change how I integrate?

Only the compliance step. Onboarding, collection, the payment lifecycle, webhooks, settlement, and refunds are identical whatever the purpose code. See [Collection Overview](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/collection-overview).

### Who are the parties in an integration?

| Term | Who it is |
| :--- | :--- |
| **PSP** | Integrator that onboards sub-merchants and calls the API for them. EximPe settles their collections to the PSP. |
| **Sub-merchant** | A merchant onboarded by a PSP. Collections are tracked against it; its PSP settles the money onward to it. |
| **Direct merchant** | A merchant with no parent PSP. Calls the API for itself and is settled directly by EximPe. |
| **Remitter (Buyer)** | The person or business in India paying you. For LRS payments they are identified by their **PAN**. |

As a PSP you onboard the businesses you collect for as sub-merchants. Collections and balances are tracked per sub-merchant, and EximPe settles them to you; you pay each sub-merchant onward.

### What are the requirements to integrate?

A merchant account with the VBA feature enabled, your `X-Client-ID` and `X-Client-Secret`, and a public HTTPS webhook endpoint. PSP callers additionally send `X-Merchant-ID` naming the sub-merchant the payment belongs to.

### What comes out of the rupees before I am paid?

Two different things, and only one of them is a cost to you. Both come out of the **rupees**, before conversion.

**What EximPe charges**

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **MDR** | Our processing fee. Your rate is agreed with your KAM. |
| **GST on the MDR** | Indian tax on that fee. |

**What passes through**

On LRS payments we also take out **TCS**. This is not our charge and we do not keep it — we hand it to the bank, which pays it to the government against the **buyer's** PAN. The buyer then claims it back as a tax credit; it shows up in their Form 26AS.

<Note>
  Quote the TCS and add it to what the buyer transfers, and it costs you nothing — the buyer funds their own tax. See [How is TCS calculated?](#how-is-tcs-calculated)
</Note>

### How do I get started?

- If you already have an EximPe account, contact your KAM to enable this rail.
- If you're new, register on [EximPe](https://merchant.eximpe.com) and complete onboarding.\

## Virtual Bank Accounts

### Is a VBA tied to one buyer?

Not by default. One account accepts money from any number of buyers, so a credit landing does not by itself tell you who paid — you tell us that during verification.

If you want an account to accept money from one buyer only, lock it when you create it. See [Can I restrict who pays into a VBA?](#can-i-restrict-who-pays-into-a-vba)

### Can I create more than one VBA?

Yes. Create as many as you want — there is no limit.

### Can I restrict who pays into a VBA?

Yes, optionally, at creation time. A **remitter lock** restricts the VBA to a specific list of remitter bank accounts (account number + IFSC); an **amount lock** restricts transfers to an amount range. The two locks are independent, and when omitted they impose no restriction.

### Can I test without a real bank transfer?

Yes. In sandbox, [Simulate VBA Transfer](/api-reference/v3/vba/simulate-transfer) triggers the end-to-end flow — payment creation, webhooks, and verification. Each `utr` must be unique across simulations. The endpoint is not available in production.

## Payments, Settlements & Refunds

### What states does a payment move through?

| State | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `ACTION_REQUIRED` | Money received; compliance details not yet supplied (or something is missing). |
| `UNDER_REVIEW` | Verification submitted; awaiting bank processing. For LRS, the amount is posted to the buyer's LRS counter at this point. |
| `ON_HOLD` | Needs manual attention. We tell you the reason. |
| `SETTLED` | Funds settled to the beneficiary. |

### The buyer paid less than the amount due. What now?

The payment waits with the outstanding amount. The buyer tops up into the **same VBA**, and you submit the top-up **linked** to the original by passing `linked_payment_id` and a `reason` (`REMAINING_AMOUNT` or `TCS_PAYMENT`). `reason` is only valid together with `linked_payment_id`.

### What exchange rate do I get?

The rate at the time we settle, not the rate at the time your buyer paid.

You collect in rupees. When the payment clears, we take the [deductions](#what-comes-out-of-the-rupees-before-i-am-paid) out of the rupees, convert what is left to your settlement currency at the rate applying on the settlement date, and send it to your bank abroad.

So the gap between the buyer paying and the money settling is exposure you carry. If you quote your customer a price in your own currency, price that gap in.

### How do I get settlement details?

Via the **EximPe Dashboard** or the settlement APIs — [List Settlements](/api-reference/v3/settlement/list), [Get Settlement](/api-reference/v3/settlement/get), and [Settlement Reconciliation](/api-reference/v3/settlement/recon), which returns the captures, refunds, and chargebacks making up a settlement.

### How are refunds funded?

A refund is netted against your upcoming settlements. We track a balance for each merchant, and the refund comes out of collections we have not settled to you yet.

If that balance does not cover the refund, the call fails with an error. Nothing is queued and nothing retries on its own — collect more, then call again.

### Where does a refund go, and can I refund partially?

A refund goes back to the remitter's **own account**. A payment can take more than one partial refund over its life.

## Webhooks

### Which events does EximPe send?

Merchant Approved, Payment Successful (money was collected and a payment created), Payment Failed, Payment Settled, Payment Refunded, and refund/dispute updates.

### How do I verify a webhook is genuine?

Each webhook is signed with an HMAC-SHA256 signature in the `X-Webhook-Signature` header, using your Encryption Key from the Developer section of the dashboard. See [Webhooks](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/webhooks) for code examples in Node.js, Python, and PHP.

### What happens if my endpoint is down?

EximPe retries: the 1st attempt is immediate, followed by retries at **1 minute**, **5 minutes**, **15 minutes**, and **1 hour**. After 5 failed attempts, EximPe stops retrying.

## LRS — education and travel payments from individuals

<Info>
  **LRS** is the Liberalised Remittance Scheme — the RBI rules that apply when an **individual** in India sends money abroad, for things like education fees or travel. It does not apply to businesses paying businesses.

  EximPe supports **education** payments today. The full rules live in [LRS & Compliance](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/lrs-compliance); the API walk-through is [LRS Verification](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/lrs-verification).
</Info>

### Who is allowed to pay under LRS?

Resident individuals only, including minors through a guardian. Not companies, partnerships, HUFs, trusts, or non-residents.

### What are the limits?

₹25,00,000 per payment, and USD 250,000 per person per financial year across every bank they use — not just payments through us. We refuse anything that would breach the annual cap on the data we can see.

### Which purpose codes are supported?

`S0305` and `S1107`, both for education from the buyer's own funds. See [LRS & Compliance](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/lrs-compliance#purpose-codes-and-documents) for what each covers.

### Can a parent pay for a student?

Yes, and then **the parent is the remitter** — their PAN, their account, their LRS limit. Set `relation` to something other than `SELF` and include `primary_person` for the student. See [Can a parent pay?](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/lrs-compliance#what-we-check)

### Why must the money come from the remitter's own account?

LRS is reported against the PAN of the account the money came out of, so a payment always settles against whoever actually paid. Enforce own-account payment in your UX.

### The credit shows a sender bank account. Is that the remitter?

It should be. You tell us the remitter's PAN and we check it against the name on the sending account. If they do not match the payment goes **on hold**, and you either refund it or give us the PAN of whoever actually paid — in which case it settles against them and their LRS limit.

### How is TCS calculated?

2% on whatever the buyer has sent abroad above ₹10,00,000 in a financial year. The first ₹10 lakh each year is TCS-free. [Quote LRS Amount](/api-reference/v3/lrs/quote) returns the exact figure — collect it along with the principal, and it passes through to the government against the buyer's PAN.

Full mechanics, including the `tcs_threshold_breached` flag, are in [LRS & Compliance](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/lrs-compliance#tax-at-source-tcs).

### Do I have to call the quote API?

Optional, but call it. Quoting reserves nothing and can be re-run freely. Show the buyer the tax-inclusive total before they pay, or they underpay and you handle a shortfall.

### What declaration does the buyer affirm?

One, covering resident status, the LRS limit, and source of funds. It is a single field, `buyer_declaration`, and the exact wording to show the payer comes back as its `label` from [Get Verification Requirements](/api-reference/v3/lrs/verification-requirements) — read it from there rather than hardcoding it.

### Is there a deadline?

Yes. Every LRS payment carries a verification expiry. Miss it and the payment expires, which is terminal — the money goes back to the buyer and nothing you send afterwards reopens it. Surface the date in your own UI and chase against it.

### Why verify the PAN before quoting?

An inactive, non-individual, or name-mismatched PAN is a guaranteed bank rejection later. [Verify PAN](/api-reference/v3/lrs/verify-pan) catches it upfront and tells you why.

Residency cannot be proved by PAN alone — non-residents hold individual PANs too — which is why the buyer also affirms residency in the declaration.

### Which documents are required?

It depends on the purpose code, and the authoritative answer for a given payment comes from [Get Verification Requirements](/api-reference/v3/lrs/verification-requirements) — do not hardcode a list. For the education codes, see the table in [LRS & Compliance](/integration-guide/v3/web-integration/lrs-compliance#purpose-codes-and-documents).

No scanned PAN card is needed — PAN is a field, validated electronically.

### When do I upload documents?

Up front, before you submit, and not tied to a payment. Each [upload](/api-reference/v3/lrs/upload-document) returns a `ref` you pass in the submit call.

### What happens if a document is rejected?

We tell you exactly what is wrong. Re-submit only that item and it goes back to review. Legible, in-date documents of the right type are the main way to avoid holds.
